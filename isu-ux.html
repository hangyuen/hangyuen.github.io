Context

Organized around the office. Not around the student.

Pages were sorted by audience and by the office's own categories. That works if you already know where to look. Most new graduate students don't, so many ended up emailing the office instead.

Research question
What are graduate students really looking for, and what stops them from finding it on their own?

What students ask

    Limitations & reflection

    What this research can and can't say. And what I'd do differently.

    Sample
    25 students shows patterns, not percentages
    Enough to find recurring themes; the ranking of needs is directional.
    Who I heard from
    Mostly students who reached out
    Students who never contacted the office may have different needs.
    Evidence
    No formal before/after metrics
    Impact is shown through what shipped, not measured change.
    Benchmark
    Public pages, reviewed in 2026
    Peer scores reflect main funding pages only, as they appear today.
    Next time, I would
    1. Recruit wider with a short survey to all grad students, not just those who came to the office.
    2. Set a baseline by counting repeat emails and page views before launch.
    3. Test early with 5 students on a prototype, then again after launch.
