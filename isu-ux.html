Context

A website organized around the office, not around the student.

Pages were sorted by audience and by the office's own categories. That works if you already know where to look. Most new graduate students don't.

Research question
What are graduate students really looking for, and what stops them from finding it on their own?

Key insights

Students weren't failing to search. The answers were split across offices.

  1. 01
    One question, four places to look
    Funding info was spread across the Grad College, departments, the Catalog, and ISSO.
    Question logSite audit
  2. 02
    Audience menus don't fit everyone
    International students need visa and funding info both before and after they enroll.
    ConversationsSite audit
  3. 03
    "Workday or PDF?"
    Students knew which form they needed, but not which system to open.
    Question log
  4. 04
    Menus must work without a mouse
    Keyboard and screen-reader users need the highlighted item and open menu to stay in sync.
    Keyboard walkthrough
From research to design

Organize around the student's task, not the office's structure.

From insight 01
One funding hub
What's available, what it covers, and how to apply, in one place.
From insights 02 & 03
"I want to…" entry points
Task shortcuts for every student, with forms labeled Workday or PDF.
From insight 04
Accessible menus
Keyboard and screen-reader friendly navigation.
Outcome

What I shipped.

I designed and developed all four areas, with my supervisor guiding priorities and reviewing changes before launch.

  • Funding pagesOrganized around what students actually asked.
  • Student FormsEvery form clearly labeled Workday or PDF.
  • International student infoClear paths connecting to ISSO rules.
  • Navigation & menusClearer labels, keyboard-friendly.
The biggest lesson
Students rarely say "I can't find the page." They say "Is there funding for me?" The problem wasn't search. It was how the information was split up.
